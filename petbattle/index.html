Pokemax

Pokemax Battles

Eight types, 62 moves, five status conditions, and multipliers that stack hard enough to end a fight in one hit.

The type chart

Eight types. Super effective is ×2, resisted is ×½, and there is no immunity — everything can hurt everything. Read a row as: what this type deals, what it is weak to, and what it shrugs off.

Rock is the offensive anomaly: it hits Fire, Ice and Flying for ×2 and nothing resists it — every Rock move lands at full value or better. But it is no fortress: Water and Grass both hit Rock for ×2, so lead with it, do not tank with it. It is also the only type two regions breed — Starter Ruins and Bandit Caves.

Dark is the defensive anomaly: nothing hits Dark for ×2, and Dark resists both itself and Ghost. Paired with the closed Ghost/Dark loop — Ghost only beats Ghost, Dark only beats Ghost — a Dark Pokemax walks through the Mist Forest unopposed.

Damage

A hit starts from the move's power, your Pokemax's attack and the target's defence, and is then multiplied:

MultiplierValueWhen
STAB×1.5The move's type matches the Pokemax's type
Super effective×2See the chart above
Resisted×0.5See the chart above
Critical×1.56% chance, every hit

Pokemax battles are short and decisive by design — every turn is a message in a channel, so fights are tuned to end rather than grind. A super-effective STAB finisher can knock out a frail same-level foe in one hit and a bulky one in two. Type matchups decide fights outright instead of shaving percentages.

Stack them and it compounds fast: STAB × super effective × crit = ×4.5. Bringing the right type is worth more than bringing the better Pokemax.

Moves

62 moves — 50 that deal damage and 12 that do not. Every move has limited PP, and when a Pokemax runs out of everything it is reduced to Struggle (40 power, typeless). Moves carry priority: a priority move goes first regardless of speed, which is how a slow Pokemax steals a kill.

A Pokemax holds four, and they are the last four it learned. Browse any species' full learnset in the Pokemax Lab.

Building a team

Build for coverage, not for power. Three Pokemax, three types that cover each other. A team of three Fire Pokemax loses to one Water wall.

Rock is the strongest opener — nothing resists it and it comes out of the beginner region — but keep a second Pokemax for the Water and Grass types that hit it back for ×2. Rock leads; it does not soak.

Bring one answer to speed. A priority move, or a Pokemax fast enough to trade with a sweeper. Otherwise the first hit is always theirs.

Between battles

There is no grace period. Whether your party carries its wounds into the next fight depends entirely on where you fight:

Where you fightYour party starts…
Safari, NPC trainers, special eventsexactly as it ended — HP and Move Uses carry over and every point of damage and every spent Move Use is saved to your roster.
Gyms, PvP, everything elsefull HP and full Move Uses, every time. Nothing from these fights is written back — win or lose, your roster is untouched.

So a long Safari is a war of attrition: chip through enough wild Pokemax and NPC trainers and your team walks into the next encounter already hurt, low on Move Uses, maybe a body down. That is the tax of staying out. Manage it and the Safari opens up; ignore it and a Lv.5 Emberpup finishes what a boss started.

Getting your condition back. Rest at a Pokemax Center — a territory building that restores HP and Move Uses, and heals more the higher its level (a high enough one patches up your whole roster, not just your active party). Or carry it with you: Potions and Revives for HP, Ethers and Max Ethers for Move Uses, all stocked from the shop. The hold caps on those items mean you cannot simply out-item a bad run.
