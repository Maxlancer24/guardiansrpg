The world

Lore

One sealed enemy, one broken mechanism, and everyone who has ever tried to fix it — how Valestia's story fits together.

The seal on Azroth

Everything in Valestia bends toward one fact: an ancient malign being, Azroth, was sealed away long ago — and the seal is failing. Not slowly, and not safely. By the group's own reckoning it is coming apart faster than the old records ever predicted. Every road, faction and favour in this world is, sooner or later, about that.

The mechanism: three pieces, and the method

The seal was built by the Varn family, and closing it again takes two things. First, three pieces — one hidden at each of three sites. Second — the part almost no one understands — the activation: the method for actually re-closing the seal once all three are in hand. Pieces without the method are useless; the method without the pieces is useless. That single fact is the axle the whole story turns on.

three seal pieces + the activation method = a closed seal

The three sites

A scroll — written in a dead script, co-authored by Gorim the Old and the Varns — names the road to the three places. Aldenmoor (ruins, north-west) is done, its piece accounted for. Veldran (a city in the mountains) is done too, and it gave up far more than a relic. Mirefall (a swamp, south-east) is the last — and, it turns out, the most important of the three.

Two families, one seal

The Varns built the mechanism and then hid it from the world — even the tunnel maps carry deliberate errors, because the builders did not trust outsiders. Maren Varn, the last of them, stayed hidden thirty years and handed you the key.

The other family is Jessie's. Her parents were not traders — they were seal-hunters, fourteen years into the exact quest you are on now. In all that time they recovered no pieces (the Aldenmoor piece sat behind a guardian they could not pass), so they solved the other half instead: the activation. Her mother wrote it down and hid it on purpose, so that no one, ever, could be forced to give it up. Then Valdris found them — and that is what killed them. Jessie was four; she was told it was a fever on the road. It was a lie Bren Valos, the Veldran senator who raised her, told to keep her out of exactly this.

Valdris

The antagonist wants a seal he can open as easily as close — that is the whole of it. Jessie's mother was the first person alive to understand that, which is why she refused his offer to work together. He has spent sixteen years collecting pieces he cannot use, because he has never held the method. He knows Jessie only by her mother's face. He buys loyalties, moves through a city's own laws, and is always a few days ahead. Soran Kael was the man he sent to talk; beaten at Veldran's west gate, Kael's last warning was that whoever Valdris sends next will not open with an offer.

Azroth is already leaking

What the Harbour Knows

Thirty feet across. The water inside it is completely still. Absolutely nobody on the docks looks at it.

A circle of dead-still water appeared in Stormbay harbour, then again at the cove where the ship made landfall: Azroth, seeping through. The same seepage into Aldenmoor births the Hollow — eyeless, humanoid things that come up from the old underground entrances and dissolve into smoke when they die. They were the first thing in this story that was not human.

Beneath Aldenmoor: the Archive

Under Aldenmoor there is something older than Aldenmoor. The refugees dug a granary foundation and found a shaft; three workers went down and one came back wrong. Below is the Archive — the Varn seal-piece, its constructs and sentinels, and the Archivist, a guardian that predates every named civilization. Forty years ago seven founders signed a compact with what sleeps down there. The Codex remembers them:

How the threads connect

The side stories are not filler — several are the same story seen from the ground. The Aldenmoor Compact is the Archive: the shaft, the missing workers, and Dren — a founder — begging you not to seal it. The Meridian Pact is someone burning forty years of Varn family records; professionals do not burn old paper, and the Varns built the seal. Cursed Castle (Aster Keep, a ruin fused with dead reactor-tech) is where the trail of missing travellers leads — the same thirty-year-old thread that runs under Jessie's past.

The rest are Valestia's own troubles — the world the seal's failure is happening to: the Ashen Hunt (Tawnholt burning one building at a time, in an order that means something to someone), Ash and Bone (a survey team lost in the Wasteland), besieged Millhaven, the gladiators of Titans' Arena, and the buried machine-sites — Agent K, the Secret Lab — where old automated defences are still very much awake.

The cast

The Guardian
You
Chosen by prophecy — Gorim's word, not yours. You carry the scroll and Maren's key, and you are the one who keeps saying the thing the others have not: that Jessie has her own face.
Jessie
Mercenary · Gunslinger
Hard on the outside, deliberately — the reputation and the distance are armour over someone loyal and kind. Raised on a lie: her parents were seal-hunters killed by Valdris, not traders taken by a fever. She wants to finish what they started, and it is not about revenge.
Bren Valos
Veldran senator · Jessie's father
The man who raised Jessie from age four and lied to her for sixteen years to keep her out of this. He spent the one thing his council seat bought him to get the group out of Veldran, stayed behind to take the inquiry, and finally called her daughter.
Garrick
Leader of the Aldenmoor refugees
Strong, charismatic, gives everything for his people, still optimistic despite the losses. He lost his brother on the second failed attempt to reach the underground stores beneath Aldenmoor.
Elric
The Scribe · Scholar
Shy, quiet, very smart; reads dead languages. He is the one quietly comparing the seal's decay against what the scroll says the rate should be — and it is faster. When he finally speaks, he is usually right.
Zoe
Jessie's childhood friend
Cheerful, fearless and forever getting into trouble. Grew up in Veldran with Jessie and knows the city — and its council receptions — inside out. Loves Jessie unconditionally, and is exactly as reliable as she needs to be at the worst moment.
Valdris
The antagonist
Wants a seal he can open as easily as close. He killed Jessie's parents for refusing him, and has spent sixteen years gathering pieces he cannot use without the method her mother hid. Reached Stormbay four days ahead of you and has never once been behind since.
Soran Kael
Valdris's envoy
The one Valdris sends to talk. Calm, exact, and honest to a fault. He offered Jessie the same alliance he once offered her mother; she refused, and put him down at the west gate. His parting warning: the next one will not come to negotiate.
Maren Varn
The last Varn
Thirty years hidden. Gave you the key. Described a couple who went to Aldenmoor thirty years ago: she had hands like a fighter and eyes that didn't trust easily; he talked too much.
Gorim the Old
Keeper of the Scroll
Guarded the scroll for years and co-wrote it. He knew who you were before you did. He has not appeared in person since.
Cara
Broker · Stormbay
Pawnshop on the South Docks, crossbow always loaded, knows everyone. She and Jessie have a history neither will explain. She stayed in Stormbay.
The Frogst Brothers
Barrel and Hammer
Hired guns who came for you personally the night of the escape from Stormbay. Jessie stayed on Pier 7 and faced both of them alone so the boat could pull away.

Where it stands now

Two of the three sites are behind you. The real prize was never the pieces — it is the activation Jessie's mother hid, and the likeliest place she left it is Mirefall, the third site, which is exactly where she and Jessie's father were headed the day they died. That is where the team goes next.

The open questions now point forward, not back: where did her mother hide the method, whether Valdris can be stopped before he finds his own way to the seal, and the quiet number Elric is keeping to himself — that Azroth is failing faster than anyone planned for.
